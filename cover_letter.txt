Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript entitled “pSMP-rnp-resource-v0.1: a frozen benchmark for low-data RNA–protein interface contact recovery” for publication in Nucleic Acids Research as a Methods and Resources article.

This manuscript is not a new general RNA–protein structure predictor, and it is not a homology-hard generalization benchmark. It is a frozen scarce-data adaptation benchmark with an auditable evaluation protocol: fixed splits and low-data subsets, disclosed evaluability, failure-aware full-cohort scoring, paired-intersection sensitivity, sequence-neighbor audits, reusable scoring scripts/checkpoints, and demonstrated multi-method harnessing (fraction-matched k-NN, contact MLP, and pairformer-only PEFT) under the same rules. pSMP is included only as a bundled reference implementation to show that the protocol runs and that low-data regimes can expose directional interface-contact signals—not as a claimed performance ceiling.

Under the primary failure-aware five-sample protocol on frozen val50, the reference implementation shows its largest gain at 10% fine-tuning (paired ΔF1 = +0.059; BH-adjusted p = 0.034), with near-flat behavior at 100% and modest absolute contact values. Post-lock temporal and cluster-disjoint companions are reported as confirmatory readouts and do not enter model selection.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been read and approved by all authors. The authors declare no competing interests.
